## How the end numbers are calculated

- **grown** = `years * rate / 100` – At 25 cm (0.25 m) a year, sixty years add 60 x 0.25 = 15 m of height.
- **h_end** = `h_start + grown` – 1.8 m today plus the 15 m you add in sixty years gives 16.8 m - the first line of the counter.
- **weight_ratio** = `(h_end / h_start) ** 3` – A body 9.33 times taller, keeping its shape, holds 9.33 cubed = 813 times as much flesh, so the weight rises eight hundred times.
- **bone_ratio** = `(h_end / h_start) ** 2` – The cross-section of a bone grows as the square of height, so at 9.33 times the height the bone is only 9.33 squared = 87 times stronger.
- **m_end** = `m_start * weight_ratio` – 75 kg times the 813-fold rise in weight gives 60 975 kg, rounded to 61 000 kg (61 t) - the second line of the counter.
- **loads** = `weight_ratio / bone_ratio` – Weight rises 813 times but the bone only 87 times, so every square centimetre of bone carries 813 / 87 = 9.3 times the load.
- **wadlow_year** = `(tallest - h_start) / (rate / 100)` – To reach 2.72 m from 1.8 m takes (2.72 - 1.8) / 0.25 = 3.7 years, so in year four you pass the tallest person ever measured.

## Reality check

- step 7: A real old town is built for 1.8 m people: the lanes are 3-4 m wide and the doors 2 m high, so a body of more than ten metres has no way down a lane. A brick wall takes only about 0.2 MPa of sideways push, far less than one leg of a 60 tonne body leaning into it, and any floor you stepped on would go first. Fantasy lets you walk through; that is exactly why the town has to be empty before you do.
- step 9: A body cannot keep its shape at 16.8 m: mass rises with height cubed (813 times) while bone cross-section rises only with height squared (87 times), so each square centimetre of bone carries 9 times the load, the heart must push blood 16.8 m up to the brain, and metabolism (mass to the power 0.75) needs about 150 times the food. Real giants stay thinner than the cube: the tallest person ever measured weighed 199 kg at 2.72 m, where the cube would say 258 kg.
- step 10: Nothing in this scenario closes the growth plates, so the extra centimetres keep arriving for all sixty years and never stop - that is the whole premise. A real 61 tonne body would crush itself long before this; here the two quay walls and the water carry you, and the canal is 12 m wide against your 16.8 m length, so you span it with about two and a half metres to spare on each bank.
