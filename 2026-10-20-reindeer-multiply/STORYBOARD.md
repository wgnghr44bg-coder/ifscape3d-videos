## Facts

- **The five largest reindeer herds on Earth peak at about 500 000 animals** – NOAA Arctic Report Card 2024, Migratory Tundra Caribou in a Warmer Climate (https://arctic.noaa.gov/report-card/report-card-2024/migratory-tundra-caribou-in-a-warmer-climate): "The five largest herds are inland (Taimyr, Bathurst, George River, Qamanirjuaq and Western Arctic), with peak herd sizes of close to or above 500,000 caribou."
- **Some reindeer herds migrate up to 5 000 kilometres a year, the longest migration of any land mammal** – Wikipedia, Reindeer (Rangifer tarandus) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer): "migrate the farthest of any terrestrial mammal, traveling up to 5,000 km (3,000 mi) a year"

## Reality check

- step 7: Reindeer are herd animals that crowd together when they cannot spread out over the range. Animals that keep arriving in the same clearing therefore stand closer and closer together, until they are shoulder to shoulder and the ones at the edge start pushing the ones behind them.
- step 9: Two hundred and fifty-seven reindeer crowd into a clearing that is only 32 metres across; the herd is about 16 metres wide, so the tents, the fire ring and the logs of the camp stand inside it and there is no open ground left between the animals.
- step 10: A real herd that stays in one place grazes the lichen, grass and shoots off the ground and tramples it: two hundred and fifty-seven animals that do not move eat the whole clearing bare over a day, while a herd on the move takes its food with it.
