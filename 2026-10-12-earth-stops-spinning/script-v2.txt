You are in a city park on a quiet morning. [pause]
Birds. Traffic. A light breeze. [pause]
You don't feel it, but you are flying east at a thousand kilometres an hour. [pause]
The trees. The buildings. The air around you. All of it, moving with the planet. [long pause]
Then. [long pause]
The ground stops. But the air does not. [pause]
A wind of one thousand kilometres an hour hits the city. [pause]
The fastest gust ever measured on Earth was four hundred and eight. [pause]
Trees, cars, rooftops. Everything not part of the rock is gone. [long pause]
The oceans keep moving too. Giant waves roll over the coasts. [pause]
Then the seas slowly drain toward the poles, and a belt of new land rises around the equator. [long pause]
The Sun stops in the sky. One day now lasts a whole year. Six months of light. Six months of night. [long pause]
But… [pause]
Earth really is slowing down. [pause]
Every century, the day gets about two milliseconds longer. [pause]
Four hundred million years ago, a year had about four hundred days. Ancient corals still record them. [pause]
And on Venus, one day already lasts longer than its whole year. [long pause]
So remember. [pause]
That quiet morning is moving at a thousand kilometres an hour. Where would you hide?
